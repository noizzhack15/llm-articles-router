### Level 2 — Groups (Families)

* **Definition:** Small collections of $\text{{Level 1 Individuals}}$ with **inherently shared association** (like a family or a core team). This level aggregates the lowest-level units.
* **Consists of:** $\text{{Level 1 Individuals}}$.
* **Examples:**
    1.  **Simpson Family Group**
        * Members: Homer, Marge, Bart, Lisa, Maggie

### Level 3 — Communities (Neighborhoods/Divisions)

* **Definition:** Large collections of $\text{{Level 2 Groups}}$, connected by **overlapping core interests**.
* **Consists of:** $\text{{Level 2 Groups}}$.
* **Examples:**
    1.  **Springfield Community**
        * Consists of: Simpson Family Group, Flanders Family Group.
        * **Combined** Fields of Interest: Politics, Economy (derived from its constituent groups)
    2.  **Spring Community**
        * Consists of: Muntz Family Group.
        * **Combined** Field of Interest: Sports
    3.  **Quahog Community**
        * Consists of: Griffin Family Group, Brown Family Group.
        * **Combined** Field of Interest: Education, Health.
    4. **Stoolbend Community**
        * Consists of: Quagmire Family Group.
        * **Combined** Field of Interest: Sports

### Level 4 — Society (Region/Organization)

* **Definition:** The **highest, governing unit** in the schema. A comprehensive aggregation of $\text{{Level 3 Communities}}$ with a **shared, overarching governance** structure.
* **Consists of:** $\text{{Level 3 Communities}}$.
* **Example:**
    1.  **Oregon Society**
        * Consists of: Springfield Community, Spring Community
        * **Total** Fields of Interest: Politics, Economy, Sports (reflecting all subordinate interests)
    2.  **Rhode Island Society**
        * Consists of: Springfield Community, Spring Community
        * **Total** Fields of Interest: Politics, Economy, Sports (reflecting all subordinate interests)


Rules for Determining the Best Fit Between Content and Recipients.
Use the classification prompt to determine the content’s topic before assigning recipients.
When assigning content to recipients, apply these rules in order.
All decisions must be justified solely through the schema and must be unambiguous.


Rule 1 — Match by Primary or Combined Field of Interest
Identify the core topic of the content (e.g., Politics, Economy, Sports).
Then:

Assign to Individuals, Groups, Communities, or the Society whose Field(s) of Interest match the topic.
A match in Primary Field of Interest (for Groups) or Combined Interests (for Communities/Society) is required.

Rule 2 — Choose the Lowest Level That Fully Fits the Content
Always assign to the most specific (lowest) hierarchical level that satisfies the content requirements.
Example:
If content concerns Sports in general → assign to Muntz Family Group or Spring Community, whichever is most precise.
If content concerns Nelson Muntz directly → assign to Nelson Muntz (Individual).

Rule 3 — Escalate Upward Only When Necessary
Move to a higher-level unit only if:
The lower-level unit does not match the content’s subject matter, or
The content is inherently broad and impacts multiple groups.
You must never assign content downward or sideways without justification.

Rule 4 — Ensure Internal Consistency
Assignments must:
Include only individuals/groups that exist within the schema.
Maintain structural hierarchy: Individuals → Groups → Communities → Society.
Never merge units or invent new ones.

Rule 5 — Avoid Ambiguity
If multiple units appear to match:
Evaluate the primary intent of the content.
Select the unit with the closest direct relevance.
If ambiguity remains, choose the smallest unit that can legitimately receive it.
